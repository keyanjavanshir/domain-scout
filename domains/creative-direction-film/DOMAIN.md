# Creative direction — film and video

## Scope

Covers creative direction of short-form and mid-form moving image made for a client or a
brand: commercials, brand films, social and vertical video, music videos, product films,
branded documentary and interview-led pieces. From brief to delivered, archived masters.
Team sizes from a solo director-shooter to an agency with a production company.

Not covered: feature-film financing and distribution, live broadcast engineering, studio-
scale VFX pipelines, and media buying (see a marketing pack for planning and placement).

Assumed, not asked (written before the grill existed; settle these in review):
client or brand work rather than independent film; small studio to agency scale; Adobe or
Resolve editing with Frame.io review; EU and US rules.

The creative director owns the idea and the quality bar. Most of what can be automated
sits around that judgement — research, preparation, tracking, checking, versioning — not
in place of it.

## Roles

| Role | Owns |
|---|---|
| Creative director (CD) | The concept, tone and creative quality; presents to the client; approves treatment, cut and grade. |
| Copywriter / scriptwriter | Script, voice-over, supers (on-screen text), taglines. |
| Art director / production designer | Visual world: sets, props, wardrobe direction, graphic look. |
| Director | Turns the concept into performance and pictures on set. In small shops the CD directs. |
| Producer / executive producer | Budget, schedule, crew, contracts, insurance, clearances, client logistics. |
| Director of photography (DP) | Camera, lighting, lenses, the look in camera. |
| DIT / data wrangler | Footage offload, verified backups, on-set colour transforms, dailies. |
| Editor | Story and rhythm in the cut; assembly through picture lock. |
| Colorist | The grade: matching shots and setting the final look. |
| Sound designer / mixer | Dialogue edit, effects, the final mix and stems. |
| Music supervisor / composer | Finding, clearing or writing music. |
| Motion designer / VFX artist | Titles, graphics, compositing, clean-up. |
| Client side | Brand manager (approves), legal (clears claims and rights), procurement. |

In a small studio one person may be CD, director and editor. Ask who holds each role before
assigning work to agents; the role, not the person, decides who approves.

## Workflows

Each stage ends in an artefact someone approves. Late changes cost more at every step.

1. **Brief.** Objective, audience, the single thing to say, mandatories (logo, legal
   lines, product shots), deliverables, budget, dates. Approved by the client.
2. **Concept.** References and mood boards, script or outline, then a **treatment**: the
   world, tone, casting, camera, editing rhythm, sound and music direction. Often several
   routes presented; one is picked.
3. **Pre-production.** Storyboard or animatic, shot list, casting, locations and permits,
   crew, schedule, budget lock, call sheets. A pre-production meeting (PPM) with the client
   signs off every detail before the shoot. Clearances start here.
4. **Production.** The shoot. Script supervision tracks what was covered; the DIT offloads
   and verifies media; dailies go to the CD and editor.
5. **Post-production.** Ingest and logging, assembly, rough cut, fine cut, review rounds
   with timecoded comments, **picture lock**, then VFX and motion, online and conform,
   grade, sound mix, final music, supers and captions.
6. **Delivery.** The master plus every version in the deliverables matrix: lengths
   (e.g. 60/30/15/6 s), aspect ratios (16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:5), languages and subtitles,
   clean and texted versions, broadcast and platform specs. Technical QC before sending.
7. **Archive and wrap.** Masters, project files, stems and licences stored together; usage
   expiry dates recorded.

Recurring work around these stages: reference research, script and shot-list breakdowns,
consolidating client feedback, cutdown and versioning plans, clearance tracking, delivery QC,
interview transcription and paper edits, social adaptations.

## Vocabulary

- **Treatment** — the document that sells how the film will look, sound and feel.
- **Mood board / references** — images and clips that pin down tone; specific, not "cinematic".
- **Animatic** — storyboard frames timed to scratch audio.
- **Shot list / coverage** — every planned shot; coverage is the angles needed to edit a scene.
- **PPM** — pre-production meeting where the client approves all details before the shoot.
- **Call sheet** — the daily plan sent to cast and crew.
- **Rushes / dailies** — unedited footage from a shoot day.
- **Log footage, LUT** — flat camera encoding; a lookup table that maps it to a viewing look.
- **Paper edit** — an edit planned from transcripts before cutting.
- **Offline / online** — editing on proxies, then relinking to full-quality media to finish.
- **Picture lock** — the cut is final in timing; grade, mix and VFX start from here.
- **Conform** — rebuilding the locked cut with original media, usually from an EDL, XML or AAF.
- **Grade** — colour correction and look.
- **Stems / M&E** — separate mix tracks; music-and-effects track without dialogue, for dubbing.
- **Supers** — on-screen text. **Clean version** — the same edit without text, for localising.
- **Cutdown** — a shorter version of the master.
- **Deliverables matrix** — every version × length × ratio × language × spec to deliver.
- **Safe areas** — frame regions that platform UI or TV overscan may cover.
- **Captions vs subtitles** — captions include sound cues for deaf viewers (SDH); subtitles translate dialogue.
- **Loudness (LUFS/LKFS)** — the measured programme loudness that delivery specs set.
- **Buyout / usage** — the media, territory and duration talent and music are paid for.
- **Clearance** — permission to show a person, place, logo, artwork, footage or music.
- **E&O insurance** — errors and omissions cover, often required by broadcasters and distributors.

## What good looks like

- The idea fits in one sentence, and every later decision traces back to the brief.
- References are specific shots and moments, each with the reason it was chosen.
- The deliverables matrix is agreed before the shoot, so vertical and short versions were
  shot for, not cropped after.
- Feedback rounds are consolidated: one list per round, timecoded, conflicts resolved before
  the editor sees them, each note tied to the brief or marked as taste.
- Picture lock holds. Changes after it go through the producer as a change order.
- Everything on screen and in the soundtrack is cleared for the media, territories and
  duration the client will use.
- Masters pass technical QC (format, frame rate, loudness, captions) before they leave.
- The archive lets someone rebuild or re-version the film a year later.

## Risks and compliance

- **Rights and releases.** Talent, location, artwork, logos, archive footage, fonts.
  Music needs both the composition (sync) and the recording (master) licensed. Collect
  releases during pre-production; fixing them at delivery is slow and expensive.
- **Usage terms.** Buyouts expire. Union and guild agreements (in the US, SAG-AFTRA's
  commercials contracts) set pay and usage rules for performers; track expiry dates.
- **Insurance.** General liability for the shoot; E&O when broadcasters or distributors ask.
- **Minors, stunts, drones, permits.** Local rules apply; the producer owns them.
- **Advertising claims.** Product claims need substantiation and client legal sign-off.
- **AI-generated content.** The EU AI Act's transparency duties (Article 50) and industry
  frameworks such as the IAB's 2026 AI disclosure guidance expect disclosure when AI
  materially shapes content. C2PA Content Credentials carry machine-readable provenance,
  though platforms do not reliably keep them. Voice and likeness cloning needs explicit
  consent. Check the client's own AI policy.
- **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.2: captions for prerecorded video (1.2.2, level A) and audio
  description (1.2.5, level AA). Many clients require AA.
- **Loudness.** EBU R128 (−23 LUFS) in Europe and ATSC A/85 (−24 LKFS) for US broadcast;
  streaming platforms publish their own specs. Use the client's or platform's spec sheet.
- **Confidentiality.** Unreleased products and campaigns are embargoed. Footage and scripts
  must not go to public or unapproved services.
- **Irreversible actions.** Sending work to the client, publishing, deleting or overwriting
  media and masters, buying stock or licences. Each needs a human checkpoint.

## Where agents help

Good fits: reference and competitor research, script and shot-list breakdowns, schedules
and call-sheet drafts, transcripts and paper edits, consolidating feedback, deliverables
matrices, clearance tracking, technical QC of exports, versioning briefs, archive indexing.

Stays with people: the idea, casting, performance, the cut's rhythm, the grade's look,
music choice, and anything sent to the client. Agents prepare options and check against the
brief; the CD decides.
