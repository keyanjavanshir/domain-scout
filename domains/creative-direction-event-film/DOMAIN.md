# Creative direction — event films and short commercials (solo)

## Scope

A one-person creative team: the creative director also directs, shoots, records sound,
edits and grades. The work is **event aftermovies** (one to three minutes) and **short
commercials** (15–60 seconds) for small-business clients, plus social versions. Shoots are
run-and-gun on one camera, often in a log profile; a project is one to three hours of
footage, a few hundred clips. Briefs are short and open: the creative director may
challenge them. Turnaround is one to two weeks.

Not covered: crewed, scripted productions and agency chains (see
`creative-direction-film`); rights, releases and other legal questions, left out of this
pack on purpose for now.

Assumed, not asked: field-standard vocabulary; final creative choices and all contact with
the client stay with the person.

## Roles

| Role | Who |
|---|---|
| Creative director, director, camera, sound, editor, colorist | The same person. |
| Client decision-maker | Often the owner or CEO, deciding directly after asking colleagues informally. |
| Freelancers | Occasionally: a second shooter, a sound recordist, a colorist. |

What a solo creative director lacks is not hands but a **second creative voice**: someone
to pitch alternatives, question the idea and say what is missing. Agents can fill the roles
of sparring partner, assistant editor (logging and selects) and music supervisor's
assistant. They prepare and challenge; the person decides.

## Workflows

**Aftermovie**, the common case:

1. **Brief call.** The feeling, the must-haves (sponsors, speakers, logo), length,
   platforms, deadline.
2. **Intention**, in one of two modes:
   - *Plan-first* (big events): a one-page vision, two or three possible structures, a
     music direction and a shot wishlist per structure.
   - *Footage-first* (everything else): shoot freely, then derive the structures from what
     was captured.
3. **Shoot.** Run-and-gun, one camera, log profile, natural sound always recording.
4. **Ingest and proxies.** Copy and back up the originals, make proxies, never edit
   originals in place.
5. **Log.** Every clip described: shot type, subject, energy, faces, speech, usable
   moments. Then selects and a map of the event's moments.
6. **Structure and music.** Pick the structure the footage best supports, and the track
   whose build and peak fit it. Place the music first.
7. **Rough cut to the music**, then a **sound-design pass**: ambience, natural sound,
   risers, whooshes, impacts.
8. **One feedback round.** Feedback often arrives as text or voice messages, without
   timecodes.
9. **Grade after the cut is locked**, then deliver the master and social versions by
   file transfer while the event is still fresh.

**Short commercial:** the same, with more weight on steps 1–2 (a concept and a shot list
before the shoot) and less on footage discovery.

## Vocabulary

- **Aftermovie / recap / sizzle reel** — a short, music-led film of an event, made to make
  people wish they had been there and want to come next time.
- **Run-and-gun** — shooting without a script or fixed setups, reacting to what happens.
- **Selects** — the usable or best moments. **Stringout** — a sequence of selects grouped
  by theme or moment for review.
- **Proxy** — a small copy of a clip for fast editing and analysis.
- **Log profile (e.g. V-Log L)** — flat footage kept for grading; it looks washed out until
  a conversion LUT (to Rec.709) is applied.
- **Structure** — the shape of the edit; for aftermovies usually build → peak → outro.
- **Energy curve** — how intensity rises and falls across the film; it should follow the
  music's.
- **Cut on the beat** — placing cuts on musical beats; on accents, not on every beat.
- **Drop / peak** — the musical high point where the most energetic footage lands.
- **Riser, whoosh, impact (hit)** — sound effects that build tension, carry a transition,
  or punctuate a moment.
- **J-cut / L-cut** — the next shot's sound starts early / the last shot's sound carries on.
- **Natural sound (nat sound)** — the real sound of the event: crowd, voices, room.
- **Hook** — the first seconds that make someone keep watching, crucial on social.
- **Vision one-pager** — intent, references, look, music direction and structures on a page.

## What good looks like

- Clients judge first by **energy and the edit**, then by **the look**.
- The film has a clear build, a peak that lands and an ending; the stronger the build, the
  harder the peak hits.
- Music is chosen and placed before cutting; the track's own shape (quiet intro, rising
  middle, peak) gives the cut points.
- Cuts follow musical accents, not every beat; the pace varies.
- Sound design is restrained: a transition sound peaks on the cut, effects mark only
  moments that matter, and natural sound gives the film air.
- Real faces and moments beat generic wide shots; a person or moment to follow turns a
  montage into a story when the footage allows it.
- A hook in the first seconds; the social versions work without sound.
- There is an intention before the footage is opened, even if the footage changes it.

## Risks and compliance

Left out of this pack at the owner's request; `creative-direction-film` covers rights,
releases and music licensing in general. Two working rules stay because they are about
how agents behave, not about law:

- **Irreversible or visible actions** — sending to the client, publishing, deleting or
  overwriting media — are always done by the person.
- **Footage stays local.** Agents work from proxies, stills and audio on the person's own
  machine; nothing unreleased is uploaded to run an agent.

## Where agents help

Good fits, in the order a solo creative director tends to need them:

- **Intention**: a sparring partner that pitches angles, challenges the brief and drafts the
  vision one-pager; or, footage-first, proposes structures from the log.
- **Footage logging**: turning a few hundred clips into a searchable log, selects and a
  moment map, which removes the "where do I even start" block.
- **Music and sound**: a shortlist of licensable tracks matched to mood, tempo and the
  structure's energy curve, a beat map, and a sound-design cue list.
- **Feedback**: turning voice and text messages into a timecoded change list.
- **Versions**: briefs for each social cut.

Stays with the person: choosing the structure, the track, the takes and the look, and
everything the client sees. Build trust in stages: start with the one-page vision in a
plain document; add visual boards or editor integration only once that works.
