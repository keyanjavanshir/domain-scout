# Questions for solo event-film systems

Add these to agent-architect's design tree. Recommended answers fit a solo creative
director making aftermovies and short commercials.

## Contents
- Outcome
- Trigger and inputs
- Decomposition
- Roles
- Knowledge
- Tools and data
- Risk and permissions
- Verification
- Environment

## Outcome

- **Which job comes first: intention, footage logging, music and sound, or feedback?**
  Recommended: intention (the vision one-pager or structures) — everything later depends
  on it, and it is the cheapest to get right.
- **What does a run hand back, and where will you read it?** Recommended: one Markdown or
  text document you keep open beside the editor; no new app to learn.
- **Is the system a sparring partner or an assistant?** Recommended: both, kept apart — a
  sparring skill that proposes and challenges, and assistant agents that log and prepare.

## Trigger and inputs

- **Which mode is this run: plan-first or footage-first?** Recommended: ask at the start of
  every run; plan-first for big events, footage-first otherwise.
- **What does the brief look like?** Recommended: a few lines you paste in (feeling,
  must-haves, length, platforms, deadline); the system asks for anything missing.
- **Where do the proxies live, and are they made before the run?** Recommended: yes, made
  by you in the editor; the system reads one proxy folder and never the originals.

## Decomposition

- **How are a few hundred clips split for logging?** Recommended: batches of about 25–40
  clips in parallel, one log file per batch, then one merge.
- **How many structures should be proposed?** Recommended: two or three, each with a
  one-line idea, sections with timings, and the clips that would carry each section.
- **Does music come before or after the structure?** Recommended: structure first, then a
  track shortlist that fits it; then the beat map of the chosen track.

## Roles

- **Should the sparring partner argue against your idea?** Recommended: yes, once per idea —
  the strongest objection from the client's point of view, then one alternative.
- **Should any agent pick the structure, the track or the takes?** Recommended: no; agents
  rank options with reasons and you choose.
- **Is a sound-design agent worth it, or does the music agent cover it?** Recommended: a
  separate cue-list step after the track is chosen — it needs the beat map and the cut.

## Knowledge

- **What makes a great aftermovie for this client?** Recommended: write it down once (for
  most clients: energy and edit first, then the look) and give it to every agent.
- **Which reference films or past projects define your style?** Recommended: three to five
  links or stills with one line each on what you like; the sparring partner uses them.
- **Which shooting profile and conversion LUT apply?** Recommended: record the camera, log
  profile and the official conversion LUT, so stills are judged on a normal image.

## Tools and data

- **Which editor, and will that change?** Recommended: integrate through exported files
  (OpenTimelineIO, FCP7 XML, EDL, marker lists) so a move from Premiere to Resolve does not
  break the system.
- **Which music library do you license from?** Recommended: one library with an official
  MCP server or API (Epidemic Sound has one), so the shortlist contains only tracks you can
  use.
- **How does feedback arrive?** Recommended: paste text and drop voice messages into a
  folder; transcribe locally.

## Risk and permissions

- **May anything leave your machine?** Recommended: only search queries to the music
  library; no footage, stills or client messages go to other services.
- **What may agents write?** Recommended: only their own files in the project's analysis
  folder; never the proxies, the originals or the editor's project.

## Verification

- **How do you know the log is good enough?** Recommended: spot-check five random clips
  against their log entries on the first runs; the log is useful if you stop rewatching.
- **How is a proposed structure checked?** Recommended: every section must name real clips
  from the log; a structure that needs footage you do not have is flagged.
- **What happens when a batch fails to log?** Recommended: retry once, then list the
  missing clips and continue.

## Environment

- **Where does it run?** Recommended: on the editing machine, next to the proxies; the
  footage is too large and too private to move.
- **How long may a logging run take?** Recommended: set a ceiling (e.g. under an hour for
  300 clips) before choosing how many batches run in parallel.
